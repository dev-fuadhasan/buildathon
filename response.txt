**Quick Answer
It is generally not recommended to sleep on your back during the third trimester, especially after 20 weeks of pregnancy.
This is because sleeping on your back can cause your uterus to compress the vena cava, a major vein that carries blood from your lower body to your heart.
Detailed ExplanationWhen you sleep on your back, your uterus can put pressure on the vena cava, which can reduce blood flow to your heart and, in turn, decrease blood flow to your baby.
This can lead to a range of complications, including- Reduced fetal movement
- Decreased oxygen supply to the baby
- Increased risk of stillbirth
- Increased risk of low birth weight
Additionally, sleeping on your back can also cause:
- Back pain and discomfort
- Shortness of breath
- Dizziness and lightheadedness
**Practical Recommendations- Sleep on your side, preferably your left side, to improve blood flow to the placenta and prevent compression of the vena cava.

- Use pillows or a pregnancy wedge to support your back and legs while sleeping.

- Avoid sleeping on your back, especially if you experience any discomfort or pain.**Important Notes
- If you wake up on your back, don't worry.
Simply roll over onto your side and continue sleeping.

- If you experience any discomfort, pain, or difficulty breathing while sleeping, consult your healthcare provider for guidance.
**When to Consult a Doctor:**
- If you experience severe back pain or discomfort while sleeping
- If you notice a decrease in fetal movement
- If you experience shortness of breath or dizziness while sleeping
Remember, it's always best to consult your healthcare provider for personalized advice on sleeping positions during pregnancy.
They can provide guidance based on your individual needs and health status.
